# Scientific Notes: Light Speed in Vacuum and Water

The speed comparisons use linear motion during each race; easing is reserved for transitions. Both light markers move at the same speed before one enters water normally, without a change in direction. The final eight-second comparison covers logical distances of 1,160, 1,044, and 870 pixels for vacuum light, the illustrative muon, and light in water.

For visible light in water, n ≈ 1.33 gives v ≈ c/n ≈ 0.75c. Refractive index depends on wavelength and conditions. The muon's chosen speed, 0.90c, makes the comparison readable and is not a universal muon speed. The animation does not use a stop-and-start absorption/re-emission model. Energy loss and subsequent Cherenkov emission are outside this scene's scope.

The γ symbol means a photon here, not necessarily a gamma-ray photon. H₂O, a drop, and faint molecule symbols identify water. The wave packet is illustrative, not a real-time optical oscillation.

References: [NIST speed of light](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-330/sp-330-section-2), [OpenStax refraction](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/25-3-the-law-of-refraction), [IceCube Cherenkov explanation](https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/glashow-event-faq/).
