# Scientific Notes: Neutrino Direction, Timing, and Ice Calibration

The first scene selects existing DOMs according to their projected distance from the muon track. Hit fractions in successive logical distance bands are approximately 93.5%, 63.2%, 18.4%, and 1.9%, with 735 selected DOMs. Brightness also decreases with distance. Flashes follow the nearest track passage with an illustrative light-travel delay; recorded hits persist during direction reconstruction. Particle labels fade where their trajectories end.

These distances are logical image coordinates (bands 0–15, 15–40, 40–75, and 75–130 pixels), not absorption lengths in meters. This is a schematic event, not real IceCube data or a calibrated detector-response simulation.

From 28.5 s, the view explains timing with animated zeros in 0.000000001 s. Two fixed strings carry three DOMs each. One muon passes between them; its rearward wavefront cone triggers six timestamps on contact. Times and engineering spacing are illustrative.

From 58.5 s, the scene explains ice shielding, transparency, absorption, scattering, and calibration with 12 LED flashers. A few penetrating atmospheric muons remain visible: deep ice reduces the background but does not eliminate it. Direct and scattered paths and a schematic pulse-time response illustrate calibration.
