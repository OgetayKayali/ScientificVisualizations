# Scientific Notes: Solar Photons and Neutrinos

The photon path represents repeated scattering, absorption, and re-emission in the solar interior. It does not imply that one original photon preserves its identity all the way to the surface. Surface light is shown separately. Most neutrinos pass through the Sun and Earth without interaction; the displayed detection is a selected rare event, not a depiction of the true interaction probability.

The detector is a generic optical solar-neutrino detector inspired by SNO/Super-Kamiokande principles, not the IceCube array or an engineering drawing of a particular facility. A recoil electron from neutrino–electron scattering produces detectable light. The PMT label points to an optical sensor, and the output graph is separated from the detector outline.

The two approaching core particles symbolize nuclear activity rather than a complete reaction equation. Sizes, distances, travel times, optical wavelengths, and neutrino flux are not to scale; oscillations are omitted.

References: [NASA solar interior](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140006649/downloads/20140006649.pdf), [SNO first results](https://sno.phy.queensu.ca/first_results/), [SNO+ solar neutrinos](https://snoplus.phy.queensu.ca/solar.html).
