# Scientific Notes: Photomultiplier Electron Multiplication

The photocathode releases a photoelectron; successive dynodes create secondary electrons. Bunches originate at different electrode positions with short time offsets, and dark boundaries keep individual electrons distinguishable. Charge reaches several positions on the anode before an output pulse appears below the tube.

The displayed bunch sizes are 4, 9, 18, 32, 52, 80, 116, 162, 222, and 300. These are illustrative counts, not the millions of electrons in a real amplified signal. The displayed gain range of 10⁶–10⁸ is not calculated from these 300 points. Electrode dimensions, particle flight times, pulse shape, and polarity are schematic; this is not a numerical PMT simulation.

Reference: [IceCube: photocathode, dynodes, and anode](https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/research/2020/02/optimizing-eyeballs-of-icecube-neutrino-observatory/).
