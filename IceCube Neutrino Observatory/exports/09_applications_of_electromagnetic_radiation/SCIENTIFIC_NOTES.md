# Scientific Notes: Applications of Electromagnetic Radiation

0–9 s: absorption lines identify a sample by comparison with schematic references. The lines are not a measured spectrum of a particular element.

9–16 s: a Sun-like star expands toward a red giant. A shape-normalized Planck curve shifts to longer wavelengths. Equal peak height does not mean constant total luminosity; evolutionary time and size ratios are not to scale.

16–24 s: a planet transits a uniform-brightness stellar disk. The light curve uses the same geometric overlap as the drawn disks, with the dip amplified for clarity. This represents transit detection rather than direct imaging.

24–28.5 s: a fixed X-ray source produces a schematic radiograph, with bones lighter than surrounding tissue. 28.5–33 s: the CT source rotates with its detector opposite, and schematic slices appear. Neither anatomy nor images are clinical data.

33–40 s: electromagnetic waves heat soup in a closed microwave oven. Warm colors indicate temperature, not visible light emitted by the food. Electromagnetic wavelengths are not drawn to scale. Scenes connect with 0.8-second transitions and contain no explanatory on-screen text.

References: [ESA absorption spectra](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2022/08/Absorption_and_emission_spectra_of_various_elements), [NASA stellar evolution](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/the-life-cycle-of-a-sun-like-star-annotated/), [NASA transits](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/whats-a-transit/), [NIBIB CT](https://www.nibib.nih.gov/science-education/science-topics/computed-tomography-ct), [FDA microwave ovens](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens).
